      {\tiny\color{knblue}\itshape Impact of Virtual Avatar Appearance Realism~[12]}\\[0.3em]

\begin{frame}{RQ3 answer}{What stylisation did to eeriness and trustworthiness, and how far it reached}
  \vspace{0.45em}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
      \panelhead{What Did Not Happen}
      \panel{%
        \item Eeriness \bad{did not fall}, it rose slightly
        \item The two avatars \hl{indistinguishable} on all five subscales
        \item No behavioural advantage either}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
      \panelhead{What Did Happen}
      \panel{%
        \item \good{No trust penalty} detected for stylisation
        \item Within a person, eeriness \bad{lowers} trust and likeability \good{raises} it
        \item About \hl{one point for one point}, on all three trust dimensions
        \item The route from appearance to trust is real, the composite just never used it}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
      \panelhead{The Dividing Line}
      \panel{%
        \item The gap is \hl{not between render styles}
        \item C2 vs C3: $0.33$ points, inside the noise
        \item C1 vs C3: \bad{$1.31$ points}, nearly three times the bound}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vfill
  \noindent\colorbox{knlight}{\parbox{0.975\textwidth}{\scriptsize\color{knblue}
  \textbf{Stated carefully:} H2 is \emph{supported}, but the two avatars are \textbf{indistinguishable rather than demonstrably equivalent}. Realised power near $0.20$ at $n=41$, so the honest claim is that no penalty was detected, not that none exists.}}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Why the two avatars scored the same}{Two mismatches inside the composite}
  \vspace{0.3em}
  \noindent\colorbox{knlight}{\parbox{0.975\textwidth}{\scriptsize\color{knblue}
  The real speaker sits far above both avatars, and the two avatars looked different to participants yet scored identically. H3 showed the route from appearance to trust was \textbf{open}. The composite changed what people saw \textbf{without travelling it}.}}
  \vspace{0.7em}

  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.485\textwidth}
      \panelhead{1. Material versus Shape}
      \panel{%
        \item The composite stylises the \hl{material}, leaving the \hl{shape} untouched
        \item Zell et al.: material is what drives eeriness~[24]
        \item But eeriness \bad{rises} when material and shape disagree in stylisation level
        \item C3 is exactly that case: a stylised surface on a photogrammetric face
        \item Stronger stylisation would only \bad{deepen} the disagreement}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.485\textwidth}
      \panelhead{2. Appearance versus Performance}
      \panel{%
        \item Both avatars carry the \hl{same} recorded voice, gaze and lip animation
        \item C3 sets a \hl{human performance} against a visibly abstracted face
        \item Cihodaru-\c{S}tefanache \& Podina: uncanniness arises from \hl{inconsistent} levels of anthropomorphism~[25]
        \item K\"atsyri et al.: face and voice realism varied independently, and \bad{mismatched pairs were rated eerier}~[26]
        \item Beyond the composite's reach: a shading operator changes appearance alone}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vfill
  \noindent\colorbox{knlight}{\parbox{0.975\textwidth}{\scriptsize\color{knblue}
  Both point the same way. The abstraction may have \textbf{removed one source of eeriness and introduced another}, and both widen as the abstraction is pushed further.}}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What this design could not show}{Two reasons the effect may exist and still not appear here}
  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.485\textwidth}
      \panelhead{The Comparison Set}
      \panel{%
        \item A real person anchors the top of the scale
        \item The only judgement left is \hl{how far below}
        \item Both avatars recognisably CG, so a \hl{rendering} difference competes with a \hl{categorical} one
        \item C3 followed C1 directly in two versions of three, C2 never did}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.485\textwidth}
      \panelhead{The Video Setting}
      \panel{%
        \item Flat screen, prerecorded, no interaction, no shared space
        \item Zibrek et al.: render style significantly moved \hl{presence} in VR~[27]
        \item Too weak in a browser window may register once the avatar \hl{shares your room}}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vfill
  \noindent\colorbox{knlight}{\parbox{0.975\textwidth}{\scriptsize\color{knblue}
  Neither is a defect in the analysis. Both say the same thing: this design tests stylisation \textbf{under conditions that work against it}.}}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Limitations}{Six, and each one bounds a different claim}
  \vspace{0.2em}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
      \panelhead{Session Length}
      \panel{%
        \item 10 to 15 minutes per study
        \item Every measure completed \hl{three times}
        \item Capped the measures and the breadth of the design}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
      \panelhead{One Configuration}
      \panel{%
        \item One composite, one avatar, one platform, three scenarios
        \item Characterises \hl{this configuration}, not NPR as a category}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
      \panelhead{Parameter Study Scope}
      \panel{%
        \item Only the \hl{highest-impact} parameter groups tested
        \item The rest fixed by inspection in development
        \item Highest \bad{among those tested}, not necessarily the best available}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vspace{0.35em}

  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
      \panelhead{Sample Size}
      \panel{%
        \item Deadline set the collection window, in the semester break
        \item $n=41$ \good{detects} a difference
        \item \bad{Not} the far larger sample equivalence demands}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
      \panelhead{Counterbalancing}
      \panel{%
        \item Three conditions need \hl{six} orders
        \item Six orders need twice the sample
        \item Three were used instead}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
      \panelhead{Version Group Differences}
      \panel{%
        \item Version groups differed on \bad{eeriness and likeability}, which is what \hl{H1 rests on}
        \item Version sets both \emph{who} and \emph{what order}, and the two cannot be separated
        \item This \bad{qualifies the H1 null}}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Conclusion}{What was built, what it showed, and what it means}
  \vspace{0.5em}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
      \panelhead{What This Thesis Built}
      \panel{%
        \item NPR stylisation for \hl{Mixamo, the Meta SDK and Avaturn}
        \item Eight techniques in sequence, each answering a limitation of the one before
        \item Narrowed to \hl{three} by visual assessment and a parameter adjustment study}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
      \panelhead{What the Study Showed}
      \panel{%
        \item The composite \bad{did not reduce eeriness} against the photorealistic baseline
        \item It produced \good{no observed trust penalty} either
        \item The evidence does not indicate stylisation reduced trust
        \item It is \bad{not sufficient} to establish that the two are equivalent}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.315\textwidth}
      \panelhead{What It Means}
      \panel{%
        \item A developer choosing between the two styles need not assume \good{an inherent disadvantage}
        \item The gap that matters is \bad{not between render styles}
        \item It is the distance to the \hl{real human}, and neither avatar closed any part of it}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vfill
  \noindent\colorbox{knlight}{\parbox{0.975\textwidth}{\centering\color{knblue}\scriptsize
  \textbf{Reaching the credibility of an actual human is a harder challenge than selecting a rendering style.}\\
  It is also the problem that decides whether virtual advisors become genuinely trusted participants rather than a passing novelty.}}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Future work}{Five steps, in the order the thesis sets them out}
  \vspace{0.6em}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.19\textwidth}
      \panelhead{1. Larger Replication}
      \panel{%
        \item Repeat C2 versus C3 with roughly \hl{120 participants}
        \item Settles the equivalence question either way}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.19\textwidth}
      \panelhead{2. Stylise the Geometry}
      \panel{%
        \item Shift proportion and feature scale \hl{alongside the shading}
        \item Tests the material-versus-shape mismatch}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.19\textwidth}
      \panelhead{3. Drop the Real Speaker}
      \panel{%
        \item Removes the human \hl{anchor}
        \item Shows whether the two avatars separate with no human in view}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.19\textwidth}
      \panelhead{4. Sweep Abstraction}
      \panel{%
        \item Replace the single point with a \hl{curve}
        \item Can reveal an optimum between full realism and full abstraction}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.19\textwidth}
      \panelhead{5. Restore Interaction}
      \panel{%
        \item Reciprocal behaviour and shared space, in \hl{VR}
        \item Across multiple avatar identities and platforms}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \vfill
  \noindent\colorbox{knlight}{\parbox{0.975\textwidth}{\scriptsize\color{knblue}
  One smaller fix: \textbf{state at the outset that the videos were not generated by AI}. Nothing in the present materials ruled out that belief, and it may have lowered trust for both avatar conditions.}}
\end{frame}
