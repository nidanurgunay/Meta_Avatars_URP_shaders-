\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Virtual reality applications increasingly rely on avatar representations to facilitate social presence and user embodiment. However, photorealistic avatar rendering can trigger the uncanny valley effect, where near-realistic human representations evoke discomfort and reduced trust rather than engagement. Non-photorealistic rendering~(NPR) offers a principled alternative, deliberately abstracting avatar appearance while preserving expressiveness and identity.

This thesis presents the design, implementation and evaluation of a real-time NPR stylisation system for virtual avatars. Eight techniques were developed iteratively: a geometry-based inverted hull outline, toon shading and its X-Toon extension, screen-space normal edge detection, Sobel edge detection with adaptive skin-to-clothing thresholding, Gaussian prefiltered Sobel, anisotropic Kuwahara painterly filtering and hierarchical multi-cue edge detection combining depth, normal and colour discontinuities. The techniques were implemented and compared across three avatar platforms: a Mixamo humanoid model, the Meta Avatars SDK and Avaturn. This revealed their visual behaviour and robustness under different mesh topologies and rendering pipelines. Avaturn was selected as the primary platform for the user study due to its facial rigging capabilities and higher photorealistic fidelity.

The user study investigates how visual abstraction affects the perceived trustworthiness of an avatar delivering advice. Participants watched short video scenarios in which a speaker recommended one of two options in a practical decision situation. The speaker was presented either as a real person, a photorealistic Avaturn avatar or an NPR-stylised avatar. After each scenario, participants rated the speaker on eeriness, likeability and three dimensions of trust. Participants registered the stylised avatar as visually distinct from its photorealistic counterpart. It was nonetheless rated no less eerie and no more trustworthy than that counterpart and both avatars fell well below the real speaker on every trust measure. Eeriness did predict lower trust within participants across all three dimensions. Abstraction and uncanny affect therefore behaved as separable properties for the composite evaluated here. This qualifies the expectation that stylisation alone relocates an avatar out of the uncanny valley.

\bigskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} non-photorealistic rendering, virtual reality, avatar abstraction, trust perception, edge detection, Avaturn, uncanny valley, Kuwahara filter.
